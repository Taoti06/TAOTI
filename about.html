About

Regulatory design backed by experience in government technology.

Intent & Practice designs regulatory operating models and represents regulators through the government technology process.

Design the regulation

Start with the harm, risk or condition that warrants regulatory attention. Understand the behaviour producing it. Decide what the regulator can influence and which interventions are appropriate.

Communication and engagement are part of that design. Education, guidance, disclosure, consultation, digital interaction and direct engagement can be regulatory interventions, not activities added after the model is built.

Design the organisation

Turn the regulatory response into capabilities, regulatory journeys, processes, decision rights, governance, workforce, information and measures.

Licensing, permissions, complaints, intelligence, inspections, investigations, compliance and enforcement have different workflows, evidence requirements and statutory consequences. The operating model must preserve those distinctions.

Design the technology

Translate the operating model into data, workflow, digital service, platform, integration, analytics and automation requirements. Technology supports the regulatory model; it does not define it.

Get it through government

Regulators often depend on a department, shared service or central technology function. Investment, architecture, cyber, procurement, data and delivery decisions may sit elsewhere.

Intent & Practice represents the regulatory requirement through those decisions so the technology remains tied to the way regulation needs to work.

Experience across the full chain.

Regulatory communications and engagement. Behaviour change. Compliance and regulatory design. Government strategy. Operating models. Technology strategy. Data and digital platforms. Investment governance. Benefits. Transformation and delivery.

These are different parts of the same problem: designing and enabling an effective regulatory system.
