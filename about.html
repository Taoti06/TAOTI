About

Regulatory design grounded in operating reality.

Intent & Practice works with regulators to define the regulatory task, design the organisation around it, measure regulatory performance and secure the technology needed to support it.

Regulatory design

We begin with the purpose of regulation: the harm or risk to be prevented or reduced and the outcome sought. We identify who is regulated, who holds the relevant duties or obligations, what behaviour is required and what influences compliance.

From that evidence, we design the regulatory response. Guidance, education, communications, engagement, permissions, monitoring, compliance and enforcement are considered as regulatory tools, selected according to the problem, risk and behaviour.

Operating model and performance

The regulatory response must be translated into organisational capability. We define regulatory journeys, processes, decision rights, governance, workforce, partnerships, information and measures.

Performance is designed at the same time. Measures should distinguish regulatory activity from regulatory effect and show whether behaviour is changing, compliance is improving and risk or harm is being reduced.

Technology and data

Technology requirements follow the regulatory model. We define the workflows, information, data, digital services, integrations, analytics and automation needed to support regulatory work and decision-making.

That is particularly important for licensing, permissions, complaints, intelligence, inspections, investigations, compliance and enforcement, where workflows, evidence and statutory decisions differ.

Government technology

Regulators do not always control the systems on which they depend. A department, shared service, central platform or enterprise technology function may control investment, architecture, cyber, procurement or delivery.

We represent the regulator's requirements through those processes and test proposed solutions against the regulatory operating model.

Experience across the regulatory system.

Regulatory communications and engagement. Behaviour change. Compliance and regulatory design. Government strategy. Operating models. Regulatory performance. Technology strategy. Data and digital platforms. Investment governance. Benefits. Transformation and delivery.

The work is different at each stage. The governing question is the same: what must the regulatory system do to produce the intended outcome?
