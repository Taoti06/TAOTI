Method

The Integration Chain.

A practical line from enterprise outcome to realised value. It prevents strategy, investment, technology and delivery from becoming separate conversations.

01

Outcome

What must change for customers, communities, government or the organisation? What public or enterprise value are we seeking?

02

Evidence

What does current performance, customer insight, risk, cost and operational evidence tell us? What is fact, assumption or constraint?

03

Capability

What products, services, processes, people, technology, data, governance and partnerships are required?

04

Choices

What should accelerate, continue, reshape, sequence differently, pause or stop? What are the trade-offs?

05

Investment

Where should scarce money and capacity go? What dependencies, affordability, risks and benefits shape the decision?

06

Delivery

What governance, accountabilities, sequencing, specialist capability and change are needed to execute the choice?

07

Benefits

What changed? Were the intended customer, service, financial, regulatory or organisational benefits realised? What should we adapt?

Integration is not central control.

The aim is not to pull every decision into one function. It is to create enough shared evidence, direction and governance for specialist functions to make coherent decisions.

Clarify the outcome → connect the evidence → expose the choices → assign decisions → deliver → measure → adapt.

Benefits close the chain.

Approval is not the end of an investment decision. Performance and benefits evidence should show whether the original assumptions remain valid and whether continued investment still represents the best use of constrained resources.

The evidence then feeds back into strategy and portfolio choices.
