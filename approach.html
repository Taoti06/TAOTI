Method

The Regulatory Design Chain.

Start with the harm or risk of harm regulation exists to prevent or reduce. Understand the duty holders and required behaviour. Design the regulatory response and operating model. Then determine the information and technology required to support them.

01

Harm & Risk

What harm or risk of harm is the regulatory regime intended to prevent, eliminate or reduce?

02

Duty Holders

Who holds the obligation? What must they do? What affects their capability and willingness to comply?

03

Regulatory Response

What mix of guidance, engagement, education, permissions, monitoring, compliance and enforcement is appropriate to the risk and behaviour?

04

Operating Model

What capabilities, people, processes, decisions, governance and partnerships are required?

05

Information

What evidence, intelligence, records and data are required to regulate well?

06

Technology

What digital services, workflow, platforms, analytics, integration and automation support the model?

07

Performance

Is regulatory effort changing duty-holder behaviour, improving compliance and reducing risk or harm? What does the evidence say about regulatory effectiveness?

Engagement is part of regulation.

Communication, education, guidance, disclosure, consultation and digital interaction can form part of the regulatory response. They also provide intelligence back into the regulatory system.

Understand risk and duty-holder behaviour → choose the response → engage → observe → learn → adapt.

Technology comes after the regulatory design.

The operating model establishes what must be enabled. Information requirements establish what the regulator must know. Technology can then be selected and designed against a defined regulatory need.

Performance closes the loop. Evidence about duty-holder behaviour, compliance, risk, harm and regulatory effect informs the next decision.
