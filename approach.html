Method

The Regulatory Design Chain.

Start with the condition regulation exists to change. Design the response and operating model. Then determine the information and technology required to support them.

01

Harm

What condition, risk or harm requires regulatory attention?

02

Behaviour

Who needs to behave differently? What drives the current behaviour?

03

Response

What mix of engagement, education, guidance, monitoring, compliance or enforcement is appropriate?

04

Operating Model

What capabilities, people, processes, decisions, governance and partnerships are required?

05

Information

What evidence, intelligence, records and data are required to regulate well?

06

Technology

What digital services, workflow, platforms, analytics, integration and automation support the model?

07

Performance

Is behaviour changing? Is risk or harm reducing? Are the interventions working?

Engagement is part of regulation.

Communication, education, guidance, disclosure, consultation and digital interaction can themselves be regulatory interventions. They also provide intelligence back into the regulatory system.

Understand behaviour → choose the response → engage → observe → learn → adapt.

Technology comes after the regulatory design.

The operating model establishes what must be enabled. Information requirements establish what the regulator must know. Technology can then be selected and designed against a defined regulatory need.

Performance closes the loop. Evidence about behaviour, harm and regulatory effect informs the next decision.
