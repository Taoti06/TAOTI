Regulatory Design + Technology

We design how regulators work and the technology they need to do it.

We begin with the regulatory outcome: the harm or risk to be prevented or reduced, the people and organisations whose behaviour matters, and the obligations that apply. From there, we design the regulatory response, operating model, information and performance measures. We then define the technology required to support that model and represent the regulator's requirements through government investment, architecture, procurement and delivery.

Start with the regulatory task

Do not begin with the system.

What harm or risk is the regulatory regime intended to prevent or reduce?

Who is regulated, who holds the duty or obligation, and what behaviour is required?

What regulatory response is appropriate?

What capabilities and decisions does the regulator need?

What evidence and information are required?

How will regulatory effect be measured?

Then determine what technology is needed.

A simple test

Can the investment be traced to a regulatory outcome?

Technology, data and delivery should have a clear line back to the regulatory purpose they support.

Regulatory Design Chain

Harm & Risk → Regulated Parties & Duty Holders → Regulatory Response → Operating Model → Information → Technology → Regulatory Performance

Each stage establishes the requirements for the next. Performance evidence then feeds back into decisions about risk, behaviour and regulatory response.

Experience

Regulation from design to delivery.

Our experience covers regulatory communications and engagement, behaviour change, compliance design, regulatory strategy, operating models, government strategy, technology and data, digital platforms, investment governance, benefits and major transformation.

It includes work inside regulators, government departments and regulated essential-service organisations, as well as the design of statewide compliance arrangements and enterprise technology and transformation.

This matters because regulatory design, organisational design, performance and technology cannot be treated as separate problems.
